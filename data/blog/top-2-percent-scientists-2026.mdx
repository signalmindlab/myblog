Md Abdus Samad of Yeungnam University, South Korea, has been included in the 2026 edition of the **Stanford University / Elsevier "Top 2% Scientists" list**, in the single-year (2025) category.

## What the list is

The list is a public database of standardized citation indicators built from Scopus data and published through the Elsevier Data Repository (version 9, August 2026 update, compiled by John P.A. Ioannidis and colleagues). Scientists are classified into 20 fields and 174 subfields, and ranked by a composite indicator, the c-score. A scientist is selected if they are in the top 100,000 overall or in the top 2% of their subfield.

The "single-year" edition looks at the impact a researcher's work had in one recent year. In this edition, that is the citations received during calendar year 2025.

## Samad's place in the list

| Indicator                  | Value                                                 |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Affiliation                | Yeungnam University, South Korea                      |
| Main field                 | Information & Communication Technologies              |
| Subfield                   | Networking & Telecommunications                       |
| Rank within the subfield   | 3,626 of 228,312 (roughly the top 1.6%)               |
| H-index / Hm-index         | 13 / 4                                                |
| Papers (indexed in Scopus) | 62, published between 2012 and 2026                   |
| Citations received in 2025 | 517 (excluding self-citations)                        |

His work is concentrated in networking and telecommunications, with a second focus on artificial intelligence and image processing.

## Why the subfield matters

The selection is subfield-adjusted. A rank of 3,626 out of 228,312 places him well inside the top 2% cutoff of his subfield. The ranking rewards citation impact, and the number of publications does not count toward the composite score.

## A note on reading the numbers

The database's authors stress that the list should not be read as absolute proof of excellence, and that not being on the list does not mean good work is missing. The figures reflect Scopus author profiles on the snapshot date, and the published version is archival and will not be changed.
